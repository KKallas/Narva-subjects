## General Information
### Objectives
The aim of the course is to provide students with comprehensive skills in parametric 3D modelling, mechanical design, and advanced additive manufacturing techniques. It introduces modern approaches to mechanical engineering, including generative design and simulation-based optimisation, while developing practical skills for solving real-world design problems in scientific, educational, and industrial contexts.

### Learning Outcomes
A student who has completed the course:
Is able to create parametric models using constraint-based design principles in Fusion 360. Design functional assemblies with various joining methods (threaded parts, bolts, adhesive joints). Apply generative design methods for optimisation, and analyse and validate designs using parametric stress simulation. Master advanced 3D printing techniques: print-in-place mechanisms, multi-directional printing, and the inclusion of inserted components. Produce technical drawings according to ISO standards with appropriate dimensions and tolerances.

### Brief Course Description
The course offers a thorough overview of the entire mechanical design workflow, from concept to physical validation. Students learn parametric modelling techniques, assembly design principles, and simulation-based optimisation approaches. In addition to the fundamentals of 3D modelling and printing, the course covers advanced manufacturing techniques, the application of generative design, and physical validation methods. A significant part of the course consists of hands-on sessions in which students design, print, and test mechanical components, culminating in the validation of simulation predictions. Through iterative practical activities, students gain the ability to create optimised designs that balance functional requirements with manufacturing constraints, while learning to use machine-learning-based generative design tools as part of the modern engineering workflow.

### Assessment
List of independent assignments and instructions for completing them:

#### Assignment 1: Basic Syringe Holder Design
Design a simple mounting clamp for attaching a standard syringe to the Dobot MG400 robotic arm. Students create a fully constrained model in Fusion 360 that holds the syringe securely while still allowing it to be easily exchanged. The design must include suitable mounting points for the robotic arm interface. Deliverables: a fully constrained 3D model, technical drawings, and a functional 3D-printed prototype.

#### Assignment 2: Print-in-Place Spring-Loaded Syringe Clamp
Design a print-in-place spring-loaded clamp for the rapid attachment and release of syringes on the dispensing system. Students create a mechanism that includes printed springs or flexure elements, a trigger/release mechanism, and mounting features, all printed as a single unit without assembly. The design must account for appropriate clearances (0.3–0.4 mm), the flexibility characteristics of the material, and printable overhangs. The clamp should allow one-handed operation for inserting or removing syringes. Deliverables: a Fusion 360 model with proper constraints, documentation of the print settings, and a functional printed prototype that demonstrates the spring action and a secure hold.

#### Assignment 3: Multi-Directional Printing Project
Design a dispensing-nozzle holder that requires a multi-directional printing technique. Students create a component that cannot be printed in a single orientation without supports, but can be optimally produced using a two-direction printing process that involves reorientation. The design should use this approach to create internal channels or overhangs that would otherwise be impossible to print. Deliverables: a Fusion 360 model, documentation of the print directions, and a successfully printed part.

#### Assignment 4: AI-Assisted Generative Design
Use Fusion 360's generative design capabilities to create an optimised structural component for the dispensing system, based on defined load constraints. Students define mounting points, load conditions, and material selections, then use AI-driven generative design tools to explore optimal shapes. The focus is on setting up appropriate design parameters and interpreting the AI-generated solutions in order to select the most suitable design for manufacturing. Deliverables: the generative design study setup, an analysis of multiple AI-generated solutions, and a 3D-printed prototype of the chosen design.

#### Assignment 5: Test Rig Design and Validation
Design and build a test rig for evaluating the generative design component under various loading conditions. The test rig should include fixtures for mounting the component, mechanisms for applying loads, and sensors for measuring deformation and stress distribution. Students use this test rig to validate the AI-generated design by comparing its predicted properties against measured results. Deliverables: the test rig design files, a testing protocol, and a report comparing the empirical test results with the generative design simulation predictions.

#### Bonus Assignment: Camera-Guided Dispensing Attachment
Design and produce a dedicated attachment for the dispensing system that incorporates a camera for precise control. The design must include:
1. An adjustable camera mount aligned with the dispensing nozzle
2. Integration with the previously designed syringe holder system
3. Light shielding to ensure consistent image quality
4. Proper cable management

Students produce a fully functional prototype, document the design principles and constraints, and demonstrate the attachment mounted on the Dobot MG400. This assignment builds on skills acquired in the previous assignments by adding the practical challenge of integrating optical components with a mechanical system.
